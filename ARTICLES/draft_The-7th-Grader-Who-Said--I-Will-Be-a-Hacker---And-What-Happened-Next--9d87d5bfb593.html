The 7th Grader Who Said “I Will Be a Hacker” (And What Happened Next)

Author : Nilanjan Chowdhury

A joke in middle school turned into a full-time obsession. Here’s the reality of becoming a hacker, breaker & builder.

I remember the exact moment.

7th grade. A classroom in Kolkata. I turned to a friend and said, “I will be a hacker haha!! I will hack you heheh.”

I had no idea what that meant.

I didn’t know what a TCP handshake was. I didn’t know what SQL injection looked like. I couldn’t write a single line of Python. To me, a hacker was a character in a movie who typed really fast, wore a hoodie, and broke into mainframes.

It was a joke. A kid saying something to sound cool.

Fast forward.

I’m 19. It’s past midnight. My notebook is open beside my laptop, covered in hand-drawn network diagrams, WAF mutation engines, and Evil Twin Wi-Fi attacks.

My GitHub has five custom-built offensive security tools. My terminal is full of root shells. I just submitted a talk proposal to speak at BSides Kolkata about AI red teaming.

The kid who made that joke in 7th grade became the thing he was joking about.

The Grind Nobody Posts About

It didn’t happen overnight. It happened through obsession.

It happened the day I stopped watching tutorials and started writing bad code.

“My first port scanner crashed on the second run. I stared at that error for an hour.”

It happened at 3 AM, manually exploiting a Linux box on HTB (TwoMillion) for three hours just to grab a user flag.

“A 3-layer bug in my own DAST scanner reported zero vulnerabilities. Zero. The scanner was lying to me, and I didn’t know it.”

I spent hours tracing the data pipeline until I found the silent failure. That night taught me more than any course did.

Being a hacker isn’t about the hoodie. It’s about understanding a system so well you can bend it to your will. It’s about building the tools the industry hasn’t built yet.

“Looking at a firewall and asking, ‘I wonder how you think.’”

That’s the whole job, really.

The Reality Check

If you’re a 7th grader reading this, or a first-year student, or someone who just thinks this field looks cool, here’s the honest version:

The joke is the easy part. The grind is the hard part.

You will spend hours staring at a terminal feeling completely stupid. You will write code that makes no sense. You will fail labs. You will get rejected from internships. You will wonder if you belong here.

“Nobody becomes a hacker by deciding to. They become one by refusing to stop.”

Keep building. Keep breaking things ethically. Document everything publicly. Someday you’ll look back at the younger version of yourself and realize you actually did it.

You didn’t just say you were a hacker.

You became one.

“I will be a hacker haha!! I will hack you heheh.”

Yeah, kid. You did.
